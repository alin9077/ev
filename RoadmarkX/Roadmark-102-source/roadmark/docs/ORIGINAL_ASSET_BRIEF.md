# Original visuals and future photography

Reconstructed implementation brief; the original named attachment was absent.

## Implemented

- Original two-bar brand mark, neutral typographic vehicle cards and seating-position schematic in `src/components/ui.tsx`.
- Schematic has five positions in two rows or six positions in three rows. Its accessible label says it is not dimensional or child-seat suitability evidence. It does not imitate a vehicle body, interior, restricted photograph or logo.
- All manufacturers receive the same graphic quality and treatment. No image generation, manufacturer image download or video playback is claimed.
- Seven original chapter illustrations in `public/media/chapters/` (desktop 1600 × 900 and mobile 960 × 1200 SVG), generated by `pnpm build:art` from `scripts/build-chapter-art.ts`. Alt text lives in `src/lib/chapter-art.ts`. They are conceptual: no manufacturer interface, figure, rating or trim detail is drawn, and the automated-driving hero keeps hands on the wheel. `pnpm build:portable` inlines them so `index.html` stays offline.
- The automated-driving chapter's flat artwork is one 10-second looping scene (`scripts/chapter-art-driving.ts`), sampled into CSS keyframes so it plays as a plain image offline. `-still` files hold the resting frame. These files are the fallback only: set `scene: false` on a chapter in `src/lib/chapter-art.ts` to return it to its flat SVG.
- Every chapter now opens on a live canvas scene instead: seven looping animations of 10 to 12 seconds, three labelled beats each, in one modernist axonometric style. Art direction is in `docs/SCENE_DIRECTION.md`. The registry is `src/lib/chapter-scenes.ts`; each scene is one file in `src/lib/scenes/`; they are shown by `src/components/scene-canvas.tsx`.
  - Day scenes sit on the page's own paper with one field of the chapter colour: price (a blank ticket, the car turning on a plinth, a year of coins, a ring and shield), range (plugging in, the battery filling under a transparent shell, then a pull-back to a map with range rings and a route), space (five seats lit, three bags loaded, a line closed round the car) and convenience (a walk from the house, the car waking and unlocking, the door opening, driving away).
  - Night scenes: automated driving (the city drive choreographed in `src/lib/driving-sim.ts`, with the camera closing in at each decision) and comfort (cool air, warm seats, a wheel over speed humps under a level line).
  - Software passes from night to morning inside its loop: an update scans the car, then a phone reaches it, then a voice waveform is answered.
  - The camera moves in every scene: it pushes in on the detail a beat is about and pulls back for the consequence. The panel's labels follow the scene from dark to light (`data-tone`).
- The car in every scene is one original, code-built model of the 2025 Tesla Model Y (`src/lib/scenes/model-y.ts`), lofted from cross-sections in millimetres to the published 4,790 mm length, 1,920 mm width, 1,624 mm height and 2,890 mm wheelbase, with public photographs as reference only. Right-hand drive, black, with blank number plates. Doors, bonnet, tailgate, charge door and mirrors move; an X-ray pass shows seats and the floor battery. No Tesla media, logo or wordmark is used. Other vehicles are generic.
- People are jointed figures built and projected in three dimensions (`src/lib/scenes/figure.ts`): hair, face, collar, sleeves, hands, shoes, a stride that matches the ground, and varied skin, hair and clothes. They are generic; none depicts a real person.
- The scenes illustrate each chapter's subject, not any car's equipment. No price, figure, rating, interface, lettering or brand mark is drawn. Panel rules are unchanged: "Conceptual illustration" label, runs only while on screen, starts paused on a resting frame for reduced motion, pause button for everyone.
- Favicon repeats the original bar mark. No promotional social-image asset was fabricated.
